# DADE principles

These are the rules we use when deciding whether a DADE design is actually about ownership or just another licensing system.

## The person owns the product

Ownership should belong to a person's DADE identity, not to one computer, one phone, or one store account.

Devices can be authorized to use owned products. Devices are not the owner.

## A store account is not the title

A store can sell a DADE product and help with downloads, updates, receipts, multiplayer, cloud saves, and support.

The store account should not be the only thing standing between the owner and the product they bought.

## Normal use should not require a live service

If a product is sold as permanently owned and supports offline use, normal use should keep working without periodic calls to Pajic, DADE, or the original store.

Some products depend on an online service by their nature. DADE should state that clearly instead of pretending otherwise.

## Rights must be clear

A deed should say what came with the purchase.

Examples include:

- permanent use
- supported platforms
- personal device use
- offline use
- lending
- gifting
- resale
- transfer restrictions
- service dependencies

A publisher should not be able to quietly replace the original ownership terms later.

## Ownership and copying are different problems

Digital files can be copied.

DADE is not built on the claim that files can be made impossible to copy. The job of DADE is to prove the legitimate ownership right and its current state.

Piracy protection can exist beside DADE, but DADE should not turn permanent ownership into an always-online rental just to make copying harder.

## Ownership should survive the original seller

Where the product can function independently, a valid deed and the material needed to use the product should remain useful even if the original store closes.

## Transfer should mean transfer

If an owned title is sold or gifted, DADE should have a clear way to show that the old holder no longer owns it and the new holder does.

How DADE 1 enforces that securely across offline devices is still an open design problem. We will not hide that limitation.

## Recovery must not secretly create duplicates

A recovery system that can create a second valid owner while the first ownership credential still works has failed.

Recovery is useful, but it has to respect the same ownership rules as normal transfer.

## Privacy by default

Ownership records should expose as little personal information as possible.

A deed should not need a legal name, email address, phone number, or public purchase history just to prove ownership.

## No forced Pajic account

A DADE identity should not require a Pajic account.

Pajic products can link a store account to a DADE identity for convenience. The account does not become the owner.

## Human language first

DADE can be technically complicated underneath. The user experience should not be.

People should see words like:

- Owned
- Digital deed
- Verify ownership
- Add device
- Lend
- Gift
- Sell
- Transfer

Protocol language belongs in technical documentation, not in normal purchase and ownership screens.
